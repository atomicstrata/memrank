# BM25 -- `bm25`

```python
from memrank.systems import BM25

system = BM25()
```

Okapi BM25: the lexical retriever the field ranks against, in plain Python.

## How it works

It keeps every document it is told in a Python list, one list per group of
[tasks](../reference/task.md), and splits the question and every document into words the same way
[`TFIDF`](tfidf.md) does -- lowercased, cut at anything that is not a letter or a digit.

BM25 keeps TF-IDF's two ideas and fixes what each gets wrong at the edges. A word occurring ten
times does not make a document ten times more relevant, so its contribution **saturates**: `k1`
governs how fast. And a long document has more chances to contain a word without being more about
it, so the saturation point scales with the document's length against the average, as strongly as
`b` says. The rarity half is `ln(1 + (N - df + 0.5) / (df + 0.5))`, which is the smoothed form --
the unsmoothed one goes negative for a word in more than half the documents.

`k1` is 1.5 and `b` is 0.75, the conventional defaults, and both are constructor arguments:
`BM25(k1=1.2, b=0.6)`. Moving them makes a run a different method, so it has to be said out loud
rather than edited into a source file. Documents scoring zero are dropped, and the rest come back
highest first.

No index, no model, no dependency.

## Why it matters

It is the strong lexical baseline. BEIR found it hard to beat across eighteen retrieval datasets,
and a dense retriever that loses to it there is a result rather than a rounding error -- so a row
with `bm25` beside it is what says whether an engine's retrieval is buying anything over thirty
years of keyword search.

Beside [`TFIDF`](tfidf.md) it measures one thing exactly: the two share a tokeniser and a store,
so the gap between their rows is the weighting and nothing else.

It runs inside your own process, and it declares that, so its latency is not comparable with an
engine reached over HTTP -- [methodology](../methodology.md) keeps the two apart.

## What it needs

Nothing. No service, no network, no key, no download.
